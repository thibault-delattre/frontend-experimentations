Noise & Texture

Flat digital surfaces read as flat because they are mathematically perfect. A little noise is the cheapest possible fix — and its real job is not nostalgia, it is breaking up the banding that 8-bit gradients produce on almost every display.

/* rasterise it ONCE to a data: URI, then use it as a plain background */

Do not leave a live filter: url(#grain) on a large or scrolling element — SVG filters rasterise on the CPU in several engines. Bake the noise into a small tiling data URI, as this page does, and it costs nothing.

5 · Which blend mode this is the actual decision

The same grain over the same gradient, seven ways. Blend mode matters far more than opacity: overlay and soft-light preserve the underlying colour and only perturb luminance, which is what grain should do. multiply and screen shift the whole surface darker or lighter and will quietly break your contrast ratios.

Rules of thumb

  • Grain belongs at 3–8% effective opacity. If you can see it as texture rather than feel it as depth, it is too strong.
  • Bake it, do not filter it. A 200×200 tiling data URI is a few kilobytes and costs nothing; a live SVG filter on a scrolling container costs frames.
  • Use overlay or soft-light so the noise modulates luminance without shifting hue.
  • Animate the transform, never the gradient. Gradient stops are paint; transforms are composite.
  • Never put texture behind body copy at a strength that reduces contrast. Test it, do not eyeball it.
