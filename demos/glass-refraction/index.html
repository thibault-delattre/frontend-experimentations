Glass & Dispersion

Physically-based transmission: light passes through the mesh, bends by the index of refraction, scatters with roughness, and splits into colour by wavelength. Drag to orbit.

The parameters, and what each one physically means

propertymeaning
transmission fraction of light that passes through rather than reflecting. 1 = fully see-through. This is not opacity: transmitted light still refracts and tints.
ior index of refraction — how sharply light bends. Air 1.0, water 1.33, glass 1.5, sapphire 1.77, diamond 2.42.
thickness how far light travels inside the volume. Drives both the amount of bending and how strongly attenuationColor accumulates.
attenuationColor
attenuationDistance		 Beer–Lambert absorption. The colour that survives after light has travelled that distance through the material — this is why thick glass looks green at the edges.
roughness scatters the transmitted ray. Above ~0.1 it becomes frosted glass; the renderer blurs the transmission buffer to fake it.
dispersion the IOR differs per wavelength, so red, green and blue refract by different amounts. This is the rainbow in a prism.
iridescence
iridescenceThicknessRange		 thin-film interference — the oil-slick and soap-bubble colours, produced by a film only a few hundred nanometres thick.

The cost

Transmission is not free. The renderer draws the opaque scene into an offscreen buffer first so the glass has something to refract, then draws the glass sampling that buffer. That is two passes, plus a mip chain for roughness. Practical consequences:

  • Transmissive objects cannot refract each other — they are excluded from the buffer they read. Overlapping glass will look wrong; it is the single most common bug with this material.
  • dispersion costs three transmission samples instead of one. Turn it off on mobile.
  • You need an environment map. Transmission and reflection both sample it; without one, glass renders black. 
const glass = new THREE.MeshPhysicalNodeMaterial({
  transmission: 1,
  ior: 1.52,
  thickness: 1.5,
  roughness: 0.04,
  dispersion: 0.45,
  iridescence: 0.2,
  iridescenceIOR: 1.3,
  attenuationColor: new THREE.Color(0x88ccff),
  attenuationDistance: 2.5,
  metalness: 0,
  clearcoat: 1,
});
scene.environment = pmrem.fromScene(new RoomEnvironment()).texture;

When to fake it instead

For a UI panel over a page, the CSS approach in Liquid Glass is orders of magnitude cheaper and needs no renderer at all. Reach for physical transmission when the glass is an object in a 3D scene, with real geometry and real depth behind it.
