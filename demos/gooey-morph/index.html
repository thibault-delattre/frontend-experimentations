Gooey Morphing

The blur-plus-alpha-ramp trick that makes separate shapes melt into one another, applied to live DOM. Move the pointer over the first panel — the cursor is a metaball.

1 · Metaballs over DOM elements

These are ordinary <div>s with border-radius: 50%. The parent's filter: url(#goo) is doing all of it. Read the alpha row of the feColorMatrix in the source — that is the effect.

2 · Gooey menu

Hover or tab into the button. Items separate from the trunk like liquid. Open state is :hover/:focus-within, so it is keyboard-accessible with no JavaScript.

3 · Organic blob

No filter at all here — just the eight-value form of border-radius (four horizontal radii, a slash, four vertical) animated between three states. Cheaper than any SVG path morph and it composites.

4 · Displaced text

LIQUID

feTurbulence generates a noise field; feDisplacementMap uses its red and green channels as per-pixel x/y offsets. Animating baseFrequency makes it flow — but it re-runs the filter graph every frame, so it is driven only on hover here.

5 · Film grain

High-frequency fractal noise, desaturated and blended overlay. Kills gradient banding on large fills for the cost of one composited pseudo-element.

Performance

  • SVG filters are not GPU-composited in most engines. Filter a small container, never body.
  • Keep the filtered subtree shallow — the whole thing is rasterised into one buffer, then filtered, on every frame it changes.
  • Text inside a url(#goo) subtree renders through the threshold too and turns to mush. Put labels outside the filtered element, or on a sibling layer above it.
