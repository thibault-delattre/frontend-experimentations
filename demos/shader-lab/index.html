Fragment Shader Lab

A dependency-free WebGL2 harness: one fullscreen triangle, one fragment shader, five uniforms. Edit the source on the right — it recompiles as you type and reports errors with line numbers.

—

The harness

Everything a fullscreen effect needs, in about sixty lines of main.js:

  • One triangle, not a quad. A single oversized triangle covers the viewport with no diagonal seam and no index buffer — and it needs no vertex attributes at all, since gl_VertexID can generate the positions.
  • Uniforms: u_resolution, u_time, u_mouse and four scalars wired to the sliders. That set covers the overwhelming majority of shader toys.
  • Hot reload: recompile on input, keep the old program if the new one fails, and print the driver's error log verbatim.

The techniques in the presets

  • Value noise → fbm. Sum several octaves of noise at doubling frequency and halving amplitude. This is the base of every cloud, terrain and marble shader ever written.
  • Domain warping. Feed fbm's output back in as its own input coordinate: fbm(p + fbm(p + fbm(p))). Turns featureless noise into flowing, organic structure. Iñigo Quílez's single most reused idea.
  • Signed distance fields. Describe a shape by the distance to its surface, then shade by that distance. Gives you resolution-independent shapes with free antialiasing, outlines and smooth unions.
  • Raymarching. March along the view ray in steps of the SDF's value until you hit something. Full 3D from a fragment shader with no geometry at all.

Reusing this

Copy main.js. Replace the preset with your own fragment source and delete the textarea — you are left with a self-contained fullscreen shader background that ships zero dependencies and costs one draw call per frame.
